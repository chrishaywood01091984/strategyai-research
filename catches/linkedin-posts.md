# LinkedIn post copy for the catch cards

Pair each post with its matching card PNG in this folder. First two lines are written
to survive LinkedIn's truncation, since that is all most people see before "see more".

House rules applied: no em dashes, framed as action rather than flagging, and no claim
that goes beyond what the card can evidence. We assembled these, we did not break them.

## 1 · Delivery Hero  ·  `delivery-hero-cream.png`

On 19 May, Delivery Hero posted a job.

Senior Legal Counsel, Antitrust and Trade Law. Berlin.

On its own, an ordinary vacancy. Three days later the first reports appeared that Uber
was studying a full takeover. On 15 July, Uber bid €41.50 a share, valuing the business
near €13bn.

Our partners had it on their board from 2 June. Forty-three days before the announcement.

We did not break that story. Every signal in it was public. What nobody did was hold them
together: the hire, the takeover chatter, the market reaction, the structure narrowing to
the regional businesses. Five separate stories, or one situation, depending on whether
anything is sequencing them.

A company does not recruit a senior antitrust lawyer in Berlin for routine work.

By the time the deal was announced, our partners had already had six weeks to position for
separation planning, remedy design and integration work. Not to read about it.

That is the difference between market intelligence and origination.

## 2 · easyJet  ·  `easyjet-cream.png`

Most firms found out easyJet was in play on 6 July.

Ours knew on 31 May.

A US private credit firm was reported to be circling on 30 May. It was on our board the
following morning, scored 65.

Then the pattern did what that pattern does. easyJet called the approach opportunistic and
the shares jumped 10%. A second suitor appeared. A third proposal at £4.7bn was rejected
and the bidder went straight to shareholders. Our confidence went 65, then 82, then 88.

An opportunistic approach into a depressed share price rarely stops at one attempt. That is
not a prediction. It is a behaviour we track, and it told us terms were coming well before
they did.

On 6 July the board agreed. A rival bid followed at £5.7bn.

Thirty-six days of runway on ownership and control structuring, slot and regulatory
analysis, and a 100-day plan across fleet, loyalty and the holidays business.

The mandate is won in those thirty-six days, not on announcement day.

## 3 · Commerzbank  ·  `commerzbank-cream.png`

A chief executive publicly rebuffing a takeover approach looks like the end of a story.

We read it as the start of one.

20 May: Commerzbank's chief executive rebuffs UniCredit in public.
4 June: the bank contacts the German regulator over the stake build.
16 June: Berlin rejects the €39bn approach outright.
19 June: the stake is lifted to 42.5% anyway.

Four events, or one behaviour. An acquirer that keeps buying through a public rebuff, a
regulator complaint and a government veto has already decided how this ends.

We put it in front of partners on 2 June at confidence 88, thirty-eight days before the
endgame, with work identified for both sides regardless of who prevailed. ECB approval
strategy. A German political and works council plan. Contingency integration across
corporate lending, payments and deposits.

Nobody missed this deal. Plenty missed the build-up.

One of those is worth being early to.
